## Delivery rule

Complete and publish **0.7.0, then 0.7.1 (patch), then 0.7.2, then 0.7.3, then 0.7.4**. The 0.6.x delivery rule applies
unchanged. Each milestone must be usable within its full stated scope through the supported CLI and
SDK, documented, validated, published to npm, and verified against its remote tags before the next
milestone is published. Do not accumulate milestones. Do not move an unfinished acceptance item to a
later milestone while still claiming the earlier one is complete. Do not label work released that
has only been implemented.

## Decisions

All four were approved as proposed on 19 November 2026. The paid-provider choice (3) resolved to
local-endpoint qualification only, stated as a release limitation, because no provider list or USD
cap was approved.

1. **Scope mapping.** Approve releasing the already-implemented live-control, summary and
   compatibility contract pieces with 0.7.0, while milestones 0.7.1–0.7.3 (now 0.7.2–0.7.4) close only on their exit
   criteria (table above). The alternative is to hide those pieces until their milestone, which
   would mean removing exports that are already accepted.
2. **Surface naming.** Proposed CLI group `akit experiment` (`validate`, `run`, `report`), matching
   `akit workflow`. SDK functions would mirror the CLI.
3. **Paid-provider qualification for 0.7.3 (now 0.7.4).** Choose either a bounded paid run (approve providers,
   models and a USD cap) or local-endpoint qualification only, stated as a release limitation.
4. **Concurrency.** Keep the manifest's concurrency fixed at 1 throughout 0.7.x (current contract),
   or schedule parallel execution as an explicit 0.7.3 (now 0.7.4) item.
   *Superseded on 19 November 2026:* the user pulled the planned 0.7.3 (now 0.7.4) parallel-execution item
   forward. A manifest may declare concurrency from 1 to 8, and live runs above 1 need an
   approval naming the level. Attempts are admitted against declared per-attempt reservations,
   so admission never commits more than a declared budget. With a budget sufficient for a serial
   run, concurrency never loses work; with an insufficient budget, which coordinates are
   measured may depend on timing, and the stop is recorded. Cost is accounted at micro-unit
   precision: each amount is rounded to the nearest micro-unit and any positive amount is
   charged at least one, so with an exactly sufficient cost budget, per-attempt rounding can
   stop a concurrent run up to half a micro-unit per attempt early (one micro-unit for an
   attempt costing less than half of one), recorded as a stop. A declared demand is an
   estimate, not a cap: with an under-declared demand and a budget that does not suffice, a
   concurrent run can overrun a budget by up to roughly one attempt's use per concurrency slot
   (one attempt serially), because every in-flight attempt may exceed its reservation before
   any of them settles. Every overrun halts the run and is recorded as `budget_exceeded`;
   attempts that settle after that halt are recorded as `halted_in_flight`. The parallel level
   and the reproducibility caveat (completion order, and sampled outputs that may differ from a
   serial run under server batching even with fixed seeds) are stated on every result and
   report.
